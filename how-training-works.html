Bhaav Tiny · our sticker video model · training explained from first principles

How Bhaav Tiny learns

A computer can't imagine a video. It can learn one small skill: "here is a slightly noisy picture, which way do I nudge it to make it cleaner?" Start from pure static, nudge it 50 times, and a sticker appears. The caption steers every nudge.

An architect (the generator) only ever draws on small tracing paper (the sketch). A translator (the text encoder) turns the client's words into notes. A builder (the VAE decoder) turns the finished sketch into the real building (the video). We hire the translator and the builder ready-made. We only train the architect.

The problem: 3.3 million numbers is too many

First principle

The generator is a transformer. Every piece of the input compares itself with every other piece. So the work grows with the square of the number of pieces: twice the pieces means four times the work.

How many comparisons per layer, for one clip?

Most of those numbers repeat each other. A yellow face is yellow across thousands of neighbouring dots, and frame 5 looks almost like frame 4. The real information is much smaller. So we compress first, and learn on the compressed version. That is the job of the VAE.

3

The autoencoder: a learned zip file

An autoencoder is two networks back to back:

video → encoder (squeeze) → small code → decoder (unsqueeze) → video again

It is trained with one rule: the output must match the input. The "loss" is how different the rebuilt video is from the original.

Why does that teach anything?

Because of the narrow middle (the bottleneck). The code is too small to store every dot, so the encoder is forced to keep only what matters: shapes, colours, where things are. The decoder learns to fill in the obvious rest. Nobody tells it what matters; it finds out because that is the only way to rebuild well.

Describing a photo over the phone in 20 words. You say "yellow round face, two tears, pink background", not the colour of every dot. The listener redraws it.

This demo just averages blocks of dots, so it gets blocky. A real decoder is a trained network: it would redraw sharp edges from the same small code. That's what training buys.

4

Why "variational"? A tidy space to generate in

A plain autoencoder only cares that its own codes can be rebuilt. It may put "crying" in one corner and "laughing" far away, with empty gaps between. Fine for zipping, bad for generating: our generator has to produce codes, and a code that lands in a gap decodes to garbage.

A variational autoencoder (VAE) adds two changes:

  • The encoder gives a small cloud (a centre and a spread), not one exact point. The decoder must rebuild from any point in that cloud, so nearby codes look alike.
  • A penalty (the KL term) pulls all clouds toward one bell curve around zero, so codes stay in a sensible range.
First principle

It is easier to learn to walk on a smooth, bounded map than on a jagged one with cliffs.

Our VAE: borrowed, frozen, used once

We use the Wan2.2 VAE reported, the same one Drishya used. It has 555M parameters and was trained by the Wan video team on a very large amount of images and video.

VAEs like it are usually trained with: a rebuild loss, the KL term, a "does it look the same" (perceptual) loss, and a second network that tries to spot rebuilt frames, which keeps them sharp general knowledge, not checked in their paper.

Why borrow it?
  • A good video VAE needs millions of clips and far more compute than our whole project.
  • It learned how pictures look in general. That is the same for stickers.
  • Frozen means it never changes. So we encode each clip once, save the sketches (the latent cache), and training never touches pixels again. Drishya calls this their biggest saving reported.

What it does to one clip

In17 × 256 × 256 × 3 = 3,342,336 Out48 × 5 × 16 × 16 = 61,440 Smaller by54× verified Time17 frames → 5 steps Space256 → 16 per side All 75,001 clips≈ 9.2 GB cache verified

It handles frame 1 alone, then every 4 frames together: 1 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 17. That is why our clips have 17 frames.

Risk for stickers judgement

It learned from real video. Stickers have flat colours and thin sharp lines. If it blurs our ticks and Zzz's, no generator can fix that, because the decoder is the last step. So we test this first (step 10, "3a").

6

The text encoder: words into numbers

First principle

A network can only do arithmetic. "Crying" means nothing to it until it becomes numbers.

A text encoder turns each word-piece into a list of numbers (a vector), placed so that similar meanings land close together.

We borrow Flan-T5-Base reported: 110M parameters, frozen, reads up to 77 word-pieces. It already learned English from billions of sentences. We only have 26,069 different caption lines verified, mostly from templates: far too few to learn language from scratch. Each caption is encoded once and cached, like the sketches.

The generator's job: from noise to sticker

Take a clean sketch x₀ and pure noise ε. Pick a noisiness t from 0 (clean) to 1 (pure noise). Mix them on a straight line:

x_t = (1 − t) · x₀ + t · ε

Show the generator x_t, t and the caption, and ask: "which way points from clean to noise here?" The right answer is the velocity:

v = ε − x₀

The loss is how far its guess is from v. This is rectified flow reported: Drishya's objective. "Rectified" means the path is a straight line, which is simpler to learn and needs fewer steps to follow.

The same thing with single numbers

Try it: drag the noise

t = 0.25

"Generate" starts at pure noise and steps back along the line: x ← x − v × 0.02, 50 times. Here we cheat and use the perfect answer for v. The real model has to guess it, and its guesses are only as good as its training.

8

Inside the generator: a transformer

The sketch is cut into small blocks: 1 time step × 2 × 2 positions. That gives 5 × 8 × 8 = 320 pieces ("tokens") reported. A transformer is a stack of layers (16 in Drishya) that lets the pieces exchange information.

Try it: hover or tap a piece

Self-attention: darker pieces are the ones the chosen piece "looks at" most. Note the same spot in other frames: that is how the face stays the same across the clip.

Cross-attention: how much the chosen piece listens to each caption word. A piece near the cheek listens to "tears"; an edge piece listens to "background". (Weights drawn by hand to show the idea.)

Trick: caption dropout → guidance reported

10% of the time, train with an empty caption, so the model also learns "a generic sticker". When generating, compare "with caption" vs "without" and push further in the caption's direction (strength 5). It follows the prompt harder.

Trick: image → sticker in the same model reported

25% of the time, replace the noisy first frame with the clean sketch of a given picture and don't score the model on it. It learns "animate this first frame". That is what our .png files are for.

One training step, repeated many thousands of times

step 0
1Load64 cached sketches + their caption vectors
2Add noisepick t, make noise, mix: x_t
3Sometimesblank the caption (10%), give a clean first frame (25%)
4Guessthe generator predicts the velocity
5Scoreloss = squared error vs the true v
6Backpropfind which way to turn each of the 212M knobs
7Updateturn the knobs a little; update the slow average copy (EMA)

Batch 64, MuonAdamW optimiser, EMA 0.9999 reported: Drishya's settings. EMA keeps a slowly moving average of all the knobs; it gives smoother stickers and is the copy we release.

How we'll score it judgement

Every clip has an exact label. So we can train a small separate checker that watches a clip and says "crying, bounce, tears". Generate a sticker for each test caption and ask the checker if the subject, motion, effect and colour are right.

Still open judgement

How many training steps. Drishya's 180,080 steps × 64 would be about 187 passes over our 61,569 clips, probably too many for a narrow dataset. We'll stop when the val score stops improving.

Words used here

Parameter
One adjustable number inside a network (a "knob"). Training adjusts them.
Loss
One number saying how wrong the model is. Training pushes it down.
Latent / sketch
The compressed version of a clip made by the VAE encoder.
Frozen
Borrowed weights we never change.
Token / piece
One small block of the input that a transformer handles as a unit.
Attention
Each piece looking at other pieces and taking in what's relevant.
Backpropagation
Working out, for every knob, which way to turn it to lower the loss.
Epoch / pass
The model has seen every training clip once.
bf16
A shorter number format that halves memory with little accuracy loss.
Checkpoint
A saved copy of the weights at some point in training.
